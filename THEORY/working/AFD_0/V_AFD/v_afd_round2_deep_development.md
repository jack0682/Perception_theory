## Part A — OP-VAFD-003 Markovianity (V-AFD-T13)

### A.1 The question, restated

Is the process `t \mapsto Z(u(t)) ∈ \mathcal{Z}` induced by SCC dynamics a Markov process? More precisely, given the field-level dynamics `u(t)` (deterministic gradient flow or reflected Langevin SDE), does its projection `z(t) = Z(u(t))` satisfy

$$\Pr[\,z(t + s) \in A \mid \mathcal{F}_t\,] \;=\; \Pr[\,z(t + s) \in A \mid z(t)\,] \quad\text{for all }s > 0,\ A \subset \mathcal{Z},$$

where `\mathcal{F}_t` is the filtration generated by `\{z(r) : r \leq t\}`?

The answer differs sharply between deterministic and stochastic dynamics.

### A.2 V-AFD-T13(a) — Deterministic case: `Z_+` is Markov

**Setup.** Field dynamics is the constrained gradient flow

$$\dot u = -P_T \nabla E(u),\qquad u(0) = u_0 \in \Sigma_m,$$

with `P_T` the orthogonal projection onto `T \Sigma_m`. This is deterministic; the natural "Markov" question is about the *induced map on vector states*.

**Augmented vector state:**

$$Z_+(u) \;:=\; \bigl(Z(u),\;\mathrm{basin\_label}(u)\bigr) \;=\; \bigl(D(u),\, K_\mathrm{act}(u),\, E(u),\, \tau(u),\, F(u)\bigr),$$

where `F(u) \in V_\mathrm{form} \cup \{\partial\}` is the formation state F such that gradient flow from u converges to `u_F^*` (well-defined by T14 Łojasiewicz Cat A), and `\partial` is a sentinel for basin-boundary points (measure-zero set; conventionally assign `F(u) = \partial`).

**Theorem V-AFD-T13(a).** The map

$$\Phi_t : \mathcal{Z}_+ \to \mathcal{Z}_+,\qquad Z_+(u(0)) \mapsto Z_+(u(t)),$$

induced by gradient flow is **well-defined** (single-valued) on the dense open subset `\mathcal{Z}_+^\circ := \{Z_+(u) : u \in \Sigma_m \setminus \partial\}`. Furthermore the family `\{\Phi_t\}_{t \geq 0}` is a **deterministic semigroup**:

(M-1) `\Phi_0 = \mathrm{id}`.
(M-2) `\Phi_{t+s} = \Phi_t \circ \Phi_s` for all `t, s \geq 0` (semigroup property).
(M-3) For deterministic dynamics, Markovianity reduces to **the semigroup property and well-definedness of `\Phi_t`** — this is the deterministic-Markov criterion.

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Well-definedness).* Suppose two field initial conditions `u_0, v_0` satisfy `Z_+(u_0) = Z_+(v_0)`. In particular `F(u_0) = F(v_0) =: F_*`. Both gradient flows converge to `u_{F_*}^*` (T14 Cat A). However, *along the way* they may differ — `u(t) \neq v(t)` for `t > 0` even though they share the same limit.

Hence `Z_+(u(t)) \neq Z_+(v(t))` is possible *in general*. **`\Phi_t` is not single-valued on `\mathcal{Z}_+`** if we use the static `Z_+(u)` defined above. This is a real obstacle.

The repair: refine the basin-label coordinate to track *position within the basin*. The natural choice is the **full field state** (which trivially makes Φ_t Markov, since u(t) is determined by u(0)). Less trivial choices:

(R-1) Augment Z_+ with a finite-dimensional "basin parametrization" sufficient to identify u uniquely within B_F. This requires basin-coordinate theory (open: depending on E's structure, basins may need n-dim parametrization, defeating the purpose of dimension reduction).

(R-2) **Quotient out the gradient-flow orbit equivalence:** declare `u_0 \sim_{gf} v_0` if they are on the same gradient-flow trajectory. Then `Z_+ / \sim_{gf}` is naturally evolved by Φ_t. But this quotient is unnatural for inverting Z.

(R-3) **Accept partial Markovianity:** `Z_+` is Markov **modulo intra-basin field evolution**. Specifically, the *long-time-limit* Z_+ is determined by the initial F(u_0): as `t \to \infty`, `Z_+(u(t)) \to Z_+(u_F^*)`. So `\lim_{t \to \infty} \Phi_t` is well-defined on `Z_+`, and the *equilibrium* vector-state dynamics is Markov.

*Step 2 (Long-time semigroup).* Define the **equilibrium projection**

$$\Phi_\infty : \mathcal{Z}_+^\circ \to \{Z_+(u_F^*) : F \in V_\mathrm{form}\},\qquad Z_+(u_0) \mapsto Z_+(u_{F(u_0)}^*).$$

This is well-defined (single-valued by T14 Cat A) and idempotent: `\Phi_\infty \circ \Phi_\infty = \Phi_\infty`. The image is a discrete set indexed by `V_\mathrm{form}`. The map `\Phi_\infty` is the *long-time vector dynamics* and is **trivially Markov** (it depends only on the basin label component of Z_+, which is conserved by gradient flow except on the measure-zero basin-boundary set).

*Step 3 (Finite-time semigroup is path-dependent).* For finite `t`, `\Phi_t(Z_+(u_0))` depends on the *specific point* `u_0 ∈ B_F`, not just on `Z_+(u_0)`. Two distinct initial conditions with the same Z_+ value give different `Z_+(u(t))` at intermediate times. Therefore *finite-time* deterministic vector dynamics is **not Markov on Z_+** — only the long-time limit is.

**Conclusion of T13(a).** Deterministic V-AFD has two distinct Markov regimes:

- **Long-time:** `\Phi_\infty` is Markov, trivially, on the discrete set `\{Z_+(u_F^*) : F \in V_\mathrm{form}\}`. Vector-state dynamics at the long-time limit is **fully determined** by the basin-label coordinate of Z_+ at any earlier time. **Status: Theorem Cat A.**
- **Finite-time:** Φ_t on the full continuous `\mathcal{Z}_+` is **not Markov** because intra-basin field positions are lost in Z_+ but matter for finite-t evolution. The non-Markov-ness is *quantifiable*: the "missing information" is exactly the basin-interior coordinates of u(0). **Status: counterexamples exist; non-Markov characterization is sharp.**

**Cat self-rating.** A for the long-time statement; A for the negative finite-time statement (counterexample-based).

### A.3 V-AFD-T13(b) — Stochastic case: lumpability

**Setup.** Reflected gradient Langevin SDE on Σ_m:

$$du = -P_T \nabla E(u) \, dt + \sqrt{2 T_*} \, dW_t,\qquad u(0) = u_0,$$

with normal reflection on ∂Σ_m (well-posed by P-F-A1 Pkg I Cat A: T-PF-A1-SDE / AR / GI / PE).

**Question.** Is the induced process `z(t) = Z(u(t))` Markov?

**Honest answer.** No, not in general. Reasons:

(L-1) The vector projection `π_Z` is non-injective (V-AFD-T9). Hence `\mathbb{E}[f(z(t+s)) \mid z(t)]` depends on the *distribution* of u(t) within the fiber `π_Z^{-1}(z(t))`, not just on `z(t)`. Different conditional distributions on the fiber give different conditional expectations.

(L-2) **Lumpability test** (Kemeny-Snell 1976; Buchholz 1994): a partition `\{A_i\}` of state space is lumpable for a Markov chain on the original space iff the transition kernel restricted to each class A_i is uniform over `\{A_j\}` for the target class. In our case the partition is `\{\pi_Z^{-1}(z) : z \in \mathcal{Z}\}`. Lumpability fails generically because the SDE drift `-P_T \nabla E(u)` depends on u, not just on `Z(u)`.

(L-3) **Weak lumpability / asymptotic Markov:** in the small-noise limit `T_* \to 0`, the SDE concentrates on basins; the inter-basin transitions become rare (Freidlin-Wentzell) and the projected process *approaches* a Markov chain on V_form. This is the AFD-T8 picture: small-T_* projected dynamics is a Markov jump process with rates `\sim \exp(-\mathrm{Bar}(F_i, F_j) / T_*)`.

**Theorem V-AFD-T13(b) (Conditional small-T_* Markov approximation).** Assume Layer-3 hypotheses (H-MORSE-Local + H-MORSE-Saddle Cat B target + P-F-A1 Cat A + T_* axiom). Then as `T_* \to 0`, the projected process `F(u(t/T_*))` (basin-label projection time-rescaled by 1/T_*) converges to a Markov jump process on `V_\mathrm{form}` with transition rates

$$q_{ij} \;=\; A_{ij} \cdot \exp\bigl(-\mathrm{Bar}(F_i, F_j) / T_*\bigr) \cdot (1 + o(1)),$$

where `A_{ij}` is the EK prefactor (`A_{ij} = (|λ_\mathrm{sad}^-| / 2π) \sqrt{|\det H_\mathrm{sad}^\mathrm{proj}| / \det H_\mathrm{min}^\mathrm{proj}}`, Layer 3).

**Proof sketch.** Combine Freidlin-Wentzell large deviation theory (FW) for SDE on bounded domains with reflected-boundary correction. Identification of quasipotential `V(u_F_i^*, u) = E(u) - E_{F_i}` along instanton paths (standard gradient SDE identity). Bovier-Eckhoff-Gayrard EK formula gives the prefactor. Convergence to Markov jump process follows by Bovier-Den Hollander-Klein (2002) metastability theory. Full proof = OP-AFD-005.

**Status.** **Conditional Theorem (Layer 3).** Cat L3 conditional. Inherits the same dependencies as AFD-T8 (which is itself Lemma Candidate).

**Caveat.** Even in the small-T_* limit, the Markov property holds for the **basin-label coordinate of Z_+**, not for the full Z. The other coordinates (D, K_act, E, τ) at time t are functions of the basin location u(t) within B_F, not of just the basin label F. So full-Z Markovianity requires additionally that conditional distributions of intra-basin field state on each fiber are time-stationary — which is the quasi-stationary distribution (QSD) hypothesis, an additional Layer-3 assumption.

### A.4 V-AFD-T13(c) — Approximate Markov in the QSD regime

**Setup.** Assume reflected gradient Langevin SDE has a QSD on each basin (Pkg I Cat A + appropriate concentration). The QSD `\mu_{F,T_*}` is the asymptotic distribution of u(t) conditioned on no basin exit, as `t \to \infty` for fixed T_*.

**Theorem V-AFD-T13(c) (Approximate Markov in QSD regime).** Assume:

(QS-1) For each F ∈ V_form, a unique QSD `\mu_{F,T_*}` exists (Pkg I Cat A under P-F-A1-PE).
(QS-2) Conditioned on no basin exit, u(t) converges to `\mu_{F,T_*}` exponentially fast (Pkg I Cat A).
(QS-3) Time scales separate: the intra-basin relaxation time `\tau_\mathrm{relax}(F, T_*)` is much smaller than the inter-basin exit time `\tau_\mathrm{exit}(F, T_*) \sim \exp(\mathrm{Bar} / T_*)` (Freidlin-Wentzell / EK ordering).

Then on time scales `t \gg \tau_\mathrm{relax}`, the conditional distribution of `u(t)` given `F(u(t)) = F` is approximately `\mu_{F,T_*}`. Hence `Z(u(t))` is approximately a function of `F(u(t))` alone (with deterministic D, K_act, E, τ averaged over `\mu_{F,T_*}`):

$$Z(u(t)) \;\approx\; \bar Z(F(u(t))) \;:=\; \bigl(\bar D(F), \bar K_\mathrm{act}(F), \bar E(F), \bar \tau(F)\bigr),\qquad \bar X(F) := \mathbb{E}_{\mu_{F,T_*}}[X].$$

In this regime, `t \mapsto Z(u(t))` is approximately a **deterministic function of the basin-label process** `F(u(t))`, which is itself Markov (V-AFD-T13(b)). Hence `t \mapsto Z(u(t))` is approximately Markov.

**Cat self-rating.** Lemma Candidate (Layer 3, conditional on QSD existence + time-scale separation). Same Cat as AFD-T8.

**Practical interpretation.** In the regime of "well-defined formations at small noise", V-AFD vector dynamics is *approximately* a Markov chain with state space `V_\mathrm{form}` (basin label) and vector observable `\bar Z(F)`. Exact rates require H-MORSE; exponential ordering does not.

| Regime | Statement | Cat | OP |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Deterministic long-time | `\Phi_\infty` Markov on `\{Z_+(u_F^*) : F\}` | A | closed |
| (a) Deterministic finite-time | NOT Markov on full Z_+ | A (negative) | OP-VAFD-003a (intra-basin parametrization?) |
| (b) Small-T_* limit | Basin-label Markov via FW | L3 conditional | OP-AFD-005 |

**Net.** OP-VAFD-003 is **partially resolved at Layer 2 (deterministic long-time case Cat A)** and **conditionally resolved at Layer 3 (small-T_* + QSD)**. The finite-time deterministic case is sharply negative (Cat A counterexample).

## Part B — OP-VAFD-004 Injectivity Loss Invisible at Layer 2 (V-AFD-T14)

### B.1 The question, restated

V-AFD-T9 establishes that `π_Z : V_\mathrm{form} \to V_Z` is non-injective. The question OP-VAFD-004 asks: *when* does this loss affect dynamical statements?

### B.2 Sources of non-injectivity

V-AFD-T9 (b) lists three:

- (i) **Aut(G)-symmetry:** F_i and F_j related by a graph automorphism g ∈ Aut(G). All SCC operators are Aut(G)-equivariant, hence Z(g · u) = Z(u). So Z_{F_i} = Z_{F_j} whenever F_j = g · F_i.
- (ii) **Goldstone families:** continuous family of degenerate minimizers, all sharing Z.
- (iii) **Topological coincidence:** distinct basins with the same (D, K_act, E, τ).

For (i)–(ii), the formations are **dynamically equivalent**: SCC operators respect the symmetry, so any dynamical statement about F_i is the same as the statement about F_j up to the symmetry transformation. For (iii), genuine information loss occurs without compensating equivalence.

### B.3 V-AFD-T14 — Layer-2 invisibility for symmetric / Goldstone cases

**Theorem V-AFD-T14(a) (Aut(G)-equivariance + Layer-2 invisibility).** Suppose F_i, F_j ∈ V_form satisfy `F_j = g · F_i` for some `g ∈ \mathrm{Aut}(G)`. Then for all Layer-2 V-AFD statements:

(I-1) `Z_{F_i} = Z_{F_j}` (vector states coincide).
(I-2) Adm(F_i, F_k) = g · Adm(F_j, g · F_k) (admissible paths transform equivariantly).
(I-3) `C_V(F_i, F_k) = C_V(F_j, g · F_k)` for all F_k ∈ V_form (vector cost is Aut(G)-invariant).
(I-4) `\preceq_D` is Aut(G)-invariant on V_form / Aut(G).
(I-5) The vector graph G_V identifies F_i and F_j as the same vertex; their orbits in G_form project to the same vertex in G_V.

**Proof.** (I-1) Aut(G)-equivariance of D, K_act, E, τ (canonical §3, all operators commute with graph automorphisms). (I-2) Aut(G)-equivariance of admissibility (P1–P4 of AFD-D6 are Aut(G)-invariant). (I-3) Equivariance of all integrands in J_V (E continuous, D Lipschitz, K_act, τ all equivariant). (I-4) Pareto preorder is componentwise on the Aut(G)-invariant diagnostic vector. (I-5) By construction of G_V (V-AFD-D11). □

**Status.** **Theorem Cat A**.

**Cat self-rating.** A (Aut(G)-equivariance is part of canonical §3 Cat A; no new hypothesis introduced).

**Consequence.** The information loss documented in V-AFD-T9(b)(i) (Aut(G)-symmetric formations) is **Layer-2 invisible**: every Layer-2 V-AFD statement that involves F_i can be transferred to F_j by applying g. There is *no* Layer-2 dynamical question whose answer depends on the F_i vs F_j choice within an Aut(G)-orbit.

**Theorem V-AFD-T14(b) (Goldstone families: Layer-2 invariance).** Suppose `\{F_\theta\}_{\theta \in S^1}` is a connected family of degenerate formation states (e.g. translational Goldstone on a discrete torus). Then for all `θ_1, θ_2 ∈ S^1`:

(G-1) `Z_{F_{\theta_1}} = Z_{F_{\theta_2}}` (constant Z along family).
(G-2) Adm(F_{θ_1}, F_k) = translate · Adm(F_{θ_2}, translate^{-1} · F_k).
(G-3) C_V(F_{θ_1}, F_k) = C_V(F_{θ_2}, translate^{-1} · F_k).

**Proof.** Identical to T14(a) with the continuous family of group elements `\{g_\theta\}_{\theta \in S^1}` parametrizing translations / Goldstone direction. □

**Status.** **Theorem Cat A**.

**Cat self-rating.** A under the canonical commitment that SCC operators commute with the Goldstone group action (this is the *definition* of Goldstone family: stabilizer is non-trivial).

### B.4 V-AFD-T14(c) — Topologically coincident case: genuine loss

**Theorem V-AFD-T14(c) (Topologically coincident: Layer-2 loss is real).** Suppose F_i, F_j ∈ V_form with Z_{F_i} = Z_{F_j} but **not** related by any g ∈ Aut(G) and **not** in a connected Goldstone family. Then:

(T-1) F_i and F_j are distinct formation states in `V_\mathrm{form}` (per AFD-D3).
(T-2) Their gradient-flow basins B_{F_i}, B_{F_j} are disjoint (each basin contains its representative; representatives differ).
(T-3) Hence the field dynamics distinguishes F_i and F_j (initial conditions in B_{F_i} flow to u_{F_i}^*, not to u_{F_j}^*).
(T-4) But V-AFD-D2 vector state Z does not. Information loss at Layer 2 is genuine.

**Proof.** (T-1)–(T-3) standard from AFD-D2 basin partition. (T-4) by hypothesis (Z_{F_i} = Z_{F_j} given). □

**Status.** **Theorem Cat A** (consequence of definitions; explicit acknowledgment of loss).

**Caveat.** It is currently **unknown** whether case (T-) ever occurs for the canonical SCC energy on canonical graphs. Construction of an explicit example with F_i ≠ F_j, no Aut(G)-relation, no Goldstone, yet Z_{F_i} = Z_{F_j}, would be **genuinely non-trivial**. Numerically, every observed vector-coincidence on the 15×15 grid is via Aut(G)-orbits (Scenario 6 of `v_afd_examples.md`).

**Conjecture V-AFD-T14(c)-conj (open).** For the canonical SCC energy `E = λ_cl E_cl + λ_sep E_sep + λ_bd E_bd` on a generic graph G with generic parameter values, every vector-coincidence in V_form is accounted for by Aut(G)-orbits (Cases i, ii of V-AFD-T9). Equivalently: the canonical Z is **injective on V_form / Aut(G)**.

**Status.** Conjecture. If true, V-AFD information loss is *entirely* captured by Aut(G)-quotient, and the right "vector formation state" is `V_form / Aut(G)` rather than V_form. Register as **OP-VAFD-004a**, severity M.

### B.5 Summary of V-AFD-T14

| Case | Sources of vector coincidence | Layer-2 visibility |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Aut(G)-symmetric | F_j = g · F_i, g ∈ Aut(G) | **Invisible**: all Layer-2 statements transfer equivariantly |
| (b) Goldstone | F_θ continuous family | **Invisible**: same as (a) with continuous group |
| (c) Topologically coincident, no group relation | Z_{F_i} = Z_{F_j}, F_i ≠ F_j, no g, no family | Genuine loss; **unknown if it ever occurs** for canonical SCC |

**Net effect on OP-VAFD-004.**

- **Layer 2 is essentially Aut(G)-invisible.** For canonical SCC, vector projection on `V_form / Aut(G)` is conjecturally injective (V-AFD-T14(c)-conj). V-AFD on the quotient `V_form / Aut(G)` is dynamically faithful.
- **Open: existence of (c)-type coincidence.** Numerical search on canonical examples did not produce one. Conjecturally none exist; proof would resolve OP-VAFD-004a.

**OP-VAFD-004 status revision:**

- (Severity) M → **partially resolved**.
- (Open part) V-AFD-T14(c)-conj injectivity on V_form / Aut(G): **OP-VAFD-004a**, severity M.
- (Recommendation) Adopt `V_form / Aut(G)` as the canonical vector domain when symmetry quotient is appropriate.

## Part C — V-AFD-T3 Lyapunov Refinement (V-AFD-T3-R)

### C.1 The question, restated

V-AFD-T3 stated that `L_w(D(u))` is well-defined Lipschitz but **not** generally monotonically decreasing along gradient flow. Under what conditions on E and w does monotonicity hold?

### C.2 Setup

Gradient flow `\dot u = -P_T \nabla E(u)`. Differentiate the loss along the flow:

$$\frac{d}{dt} L_w(D(u(t))) \;=\; \nabla_u L_w(D(u))^\top \dot u \;=\; -\, \nabla_u L_w(D(u))^\top P_T \nabla E(u).$$

Using `\nabla_u L_w(D(u)) = J_D(u)^\top \nabla_D L_w(D)` where `J_D(u)` is the Jacobian of D:

$$\frac{d}{dt} L_w(D(u(t))) \;=\; -\, \langle \nabla_D L_w(D(u)),\; J_D(u) P_T \nabla E(u) \rangle.$$

Monotonic decrease requires this inner product to be **non-negative** along all gradient-flow trajectories. Sufficient conditions:

(MC-1) **Alignment condition.** `J_D(u) P_T \nabla E(u)` is parallel (in the same direction as) `\nabla_D L_w(D)` at u.

(MC-2) **Variational identity.** E itself is (a multiple of) L_w composed with D, i.e. `E(u) = c \cdot L_w(D(u)) + \mathrm{const}` for some c > 0. Then gradient flow of E coincides with gradient flow of L_w, and L_w decreases monotonically.

### C.3 Theorem V-AFD-T3-R(a) — Variational identity case

**Theorem V-AFD-T3-R(a).** Assume there exist weights `w \in [0,1]^4` (with `w_1 + w_2 + w_3 + w_4 = 1, w_i \geq 0`) and constants `c > 0, e_0 \in \mathbb{R}` such that

$$E(u) \;=\; c \cdot L_w(D(u)) \;+\; e_0 \quad \text{for all } u \in \Sigma_m. \tag{$\star$}$$

Then `L_w(D(u(t)))` is monotonically non-increasing along the gradient flow `\dot u = -P_T \nabla E(u)`. Strict decrease holds whenever `\|P_T \nabla E(u)\| > 0` (i.e. outside critical points).

**Proof.** Differentiating (\star): `\nabla E(u) = c \cdot \nabla L_w(D(u)) = c \cdot J_D(u)^\top \nabla_D L_w(D(u))`. Hence

$$\frac{d}{dt} L_w(D(u(t))) \;=\; -\, c \, \langle \nabla_D L_w(D(u)),\; J_D(u) P_T J_D(u)^\top \nabla_D L_w(D(u)) \rangle \;=\; -\, c \, \| J_D(u) P_T J_D(u)^\top \nabla_D L_w(D(u)) \|^2 / \| \cdots \| \cdots$$

Wait — re-derive carefully. Along gradient flow:

$$\frac{d}{dt} L_w(D(u(t))) \;=\; \nabla L_w(D(u))^\top \dot u \;=\; -\nabla L_w(D(u))^\top P_T \nabla E(u).$$

Substituting (\star) gives `\nabla E(u) = c \nabla L_w(D(u))`, so:

$$\frac{d}{dt} L_w(D(u(t))) \;=\; -\, c \,\|P_T \nabla L_w(D(u))\|^2 \;\leq\; 0.$$

Equality iff `P_T \nabla L_w(D(u)) = 0` iff `\nabla L_w(D(u)) \in \mathrm{span}(1)` (normal to simplex constraint), which corresponds to L_w-critical points on Σ_m. Strict decrease away from critical points. □

**Status.** **Theorem Cat A** (under the hypothesis (\star)).

**Cat self-rating.** A under hypothesis.

**Caveat.** Hypothesis (\star) is **highly restrictive**: it requires E to be an *affine function of L_w(D)*, which is a strong structural commitment. The canonical SCC energy `E = λ_cl E_cl + λ_sep E_sep + λ_bd E_bd` does **not** satisfy (\star) for any single weight choice w: each energy component depends on u in a way that is *not* a linear combination of (1 − Bind), (1 − Sep), (1 − Inside) — the diagnostic vector D is a *coarse* statistic of u, while E is a *fine* functional.

**Therefore V-AFD-T3-R(a) is a theorem about an idealized restricted setting, not about generic SCC.** Its main use: provides a constructive design recipe — if one *wants* a SCC-like model with monotonic L_w decrease, one can engineer E from L_w.

### C.4 Theorem V-AFD-T3-R(b) — Partial monotonicity under cone condition

**Theorem V-AFD-T3-R(b).** Assume there exist weights w and a closed convex cone `K \subset \mathbb{R}^4` such that:

(C-1) `\nabla_D L_w(D) \in K^*` for all `D \in [0,1]^4` (where `K^*` is the polar cone of K).
(C-2) `J_D(u) P_T \nabla E(u) \in K` for all `u \in \Sigma_m`.

Then `L_w(D(u(t)))` is monotonically non-increasing along the gradient flow.

**Proof.** By the cone polarity duality, `\langle a, b \rangle \geq 0` for all `a \in K^*, b \in K`. Apply to `a = \nabla_D L_w(D(u)), b = J_D(u) P_T \nabla E(u)`. Then

$$\frac{d}{dt} L_w(D(u(t))) \;=\; -\langle \nabla_D L_w(D(u)),\, J_D(u) P_T \nabla E(u) \rangle \;\leq\; 0. \qquad\Box$$

**Status.** **Theorem Cat A** (under hypotheses C-1, C-2).

**Cat self-rating.** A under hypotheses.

**Interpretation.** This is a *cone alignment condition* between L_w-gradient (in D-space) and E-gradient (pushed forward to D-space via J_D). When the two gradients are *consistently aligned* — meaning the energy gradient never "fights" the loss gradient — monotonicity holds. The cone K parametrizes how strict the alignment must be:

- K = ℝ^4 (full cone): K* = {0}. Hypothesis C-1 forces L_w trivial; useless.
- K = {0} (zero cone): K* = ℝ^4. Hypothesis C-2 forces J_D P_T ∇E = 0; useless.
- Intermediate K: e.g. K = ℝ_{\geq 0}^4 (positive orthant). Then K* = ℝ_{\geq 0}^4 too. Hypothesis C-1: `\nabla_D L_w \geq 0` componentwise, i.e. all `w_i \geq 0` (automatic per V-AFD-D9). Hypothesis C-2: `J_D(u) P_T \nabla E(u) \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^4`, i.e. gradient flow always *decreases* Bind, Sep, Inside, Persist *simultaneously*.

The componentwise-negative condition (C-2 with K = positive orthant) is **strong**: it requires the gradient flow to push all diagnostic components in the *same direction*. This is a *cooperativity condition* on E and D, which may hold in some regimes (e.g. near formation completion all diagnostics increase together) but generally fails (different basins have different diagnostic profiles).

### C.5 Theorem V-AFD-T3-R(c) — Asymptotic monotonicity near a formation

**Theorem V-AFD-T3-R(c) (Local asymptotic monotonicity).** Let F ∈ V_form be a formation state with representative u_F^*. Then there exists a neighborhood `U_F \subset B_F` of u_F^* and weights `w(F) \in [0,1]^4` such that, for all u_0 ∈ U_F:

$$\frac{d}{dt} L_{w(F)}(D(u(t))) \;\leq\; 0 \quad \text{for all } t \geq 0,\text{ with strict inequality on } u(t) \neq u_F^*.$$

**Proof sketch.** Near u_F^*, both `D(u)` and `E(u)` admit Taylor expansions:

$$D(u) = D(u_F^*) + J_D(u_F^*) (u - u_F^*) + O(\|u - u_F^*\|^2),$$
$$E(u) = E_F + \frac{1}{2} (u - u_F^*)^\top H_F (u - u_F^*) + O(\|u - u_F^*\|^3),$$

where `J_D(u_F^*)` is the Jacobian of D at u_F^* (well-defined by AFD-T2 Cat A Lipschitz on Σ_m; the Jacobian exists a.e. and is bounded). `H_F` is the Hessian of E at u_F^* on T_{u_F^*} Σ_m.

**Note.** We are *not* assuming H_F is nondegenerate (no H-MORSE invocation). `H_F \succeq 0` (positive semi-definite) is automatic since u_F^* is a *local* minimizer.

For u close to u_F^*, gradient flow direction is `-P_T H_F (u - u_F^*) + O(\|u - u_F^*\|^2)`. Vector `J_D(u_F^*) P_T H_F (u - u_F^*)` is the image of this direction in D-space.

Define weights `w(F)` by `w(F) := P_+(\nabla_D L_w \text{such that alignment holds})`, where `P_+` is the projection onto the non-negative orthant. Specifically, choose `w(F)` such that

$$\nabla_D L_{w(F)}(D(u_F^*)) \;=\; J_D(u_F^*)^{\dagger,T} \, H_F \, (\text{characteristic direction}),$$

where `J_D^\dagger` is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse and the characteristic direction is the unique stable eigenvector of H_F (well-defined when H_F is generic, ill-defined at degenerate critical points).

When this construction succeeds, alignment is exact at u_F^* and continuous to U_F by perturbation. Monotonicity follows by V-AFD-T3-R(b) with K = `\nabla_D L_{w(F)}` × positive cone, applicable in U_F. □

**Status.** **Lemma Candidate Cat B.** The construction of `w(F)` uses a pseudoinverse that may have degenerate behavior at non-Morse u_F^*; rigorous treatment requires either H_F non-degenerate (H-MORSE invocation, undesired) or a more careful argument.

**Cat self-rating.** B (sketched, hypothesis-dependent).

**Note.** V-AFD-T3-R(c) is the **most useful** of the three Lyapunov refinements, because it gives *local* monotonicity near each formation without global structural assumptions on E. The cost is that the weights `w(F)` are **formation-dependent**: no single weight choice works for all of `V_form`. This is consistent with V-AFD-D9's "weights are application-specific" caveat.

| (c) Local near formation | exists w(F) with U_F-local monotonicity | B sketched | most useful; formation-dependent weights |

## Part D — V-AFD Merge Lower Bound (V-AFD-T15)

### D.1 The question, restated

AFD-T7 Cat B (resolved 2026-05-12, `op_afd_004_proof.md`) gives `C_K(K, K−1) ≥ 0.0221β > 0` for the K-stratum transition cost under H1-H4 + WS + SR conditions. OP-VAFD-010 asks: lift this lower bound to the V-AFD vector cost `C_V` explicitly.

### D.2 Vector merge cost

For a merge transition F_i → F_j with K_{F_i} = K > K_{F_j} = K − 1, the V-AFD enriched cost is

$$C_V(F_i, F_j) \;=\; \lambda_E \cdot \mathrm{Bar}(F_i, F_j) \;+\; \lambda_D \cdot \mathrm{Var}_D(\gamma_*) \;+\; \lambda_K \cdot \mathrm{TV}(K_\mathrm{act} \circ \gamma_*) \;+\; \lambda_\tau \cdot \mathrm{Var}_\tau(\gamma_*) \;+\; \lambda_L \cdot \mathrm{Len}(\gamma_*).$$

Each term contributes a lower bound:

- (LB-E) `\mathrm{Bar}(F_i, F_j) \geq 0.0221β` (AFD-T7 Cat B under conditions).
- (LB-K) `\mathrm{TV}(K_\mathrm{act} \circ \gamma) \geq 1` for merge (single K-jump from K to K−1).
- (LB-τ) `\mathrm{Var}_\tau(\gamma) \geq d_B(\tau_{F_i}, \tau_{F_j})` (path connects two distinct PDs).
- (LB-D) `\mathrm{Var}_D(\gamma) \geq \|D_{F_i} - D_{F_j}\|_2` (path connects two distinct diagnostic vectors).
- (LB-L) `\mathrm{Len}(\gamma) \geq \|u_{F_i}^* - u_{F_j}^*\|_2` (straight-line lower bound).

### D.3 Theorem V-AFD-T15 — Vector Merge Lower Bound

**Theorem V-AFD-T15.** Under the H1-H4 + WS + SR conditions of AFD-T7 Cat B, for any merge transition F_i → F_j with K_{F_i} > K_{F_j}, the V-AFD vector merge cost satisfies

$$C_V(F_i, F_j) \;\geq\; \lambda_E \cdot 0.0221β \;+\; \lambda_K \;+\; \lambda_\tau \cdot d_B(\tau_{F_i}, \tau_{F_j}) \;+\; \lambda_D \cdot \|D_{F_i} - D_{F_j}\|_2 \;+\; \lambda_L \cdot \|u_{F_i}^* - u_{F_j}^*\|_2.$$

In the minimal version (`\lambda_E = 1`, others = 0): `C_V \geq 0.0221β`, matching AFD-T7.

In the K-aware version (`\lambda_E = 1, \lambda_K = κ`, others = 0): `C_V \geq 0.0221β + κ`.

**Proof.** Each inequality (LB-E, LB-K, LB-τ, LB-D, LB-L) holds individually for any γ ∈ Adm(F_i, F_j). The infimum of `J_V` over admissible γ is at least the maximum of each term-wise infimum (the inf does not split additively, but the bound

$$\inf_\gamma \sum_k \lambda_k \cdot J^{(k)}(\gamma) \;\geq\; \sum_k \lambda_k \cdot \inf_\gamma J^{(k)}(\gamma)$$

holds when each `J^{(k)} \geq 0`, which is V-AFD-T6 (a)). Substituting each term-wise lower bound gives the claimed inequality. □

**Status.** **Theorem Cat B** (under H1-H4 + WS + SR; same conditions as AFD-T7 Cat B).

**Cat self-rating.** B (conditional on H1-H4 + WS + SR; matches AFD-T7).

### D.4 Refinement: the sum bound is not tight

**Observation.** The bound in T15 sums *term-wise* lower bounds. For a *specific* minimizing path γ_*, the actual contributions to `J_V` may exhibit *trade-offs*: a path that minimizes Bar may have larger Var_D, and vice versa.

The minimizing path attains `C_V = J_V(γ_*; F_i)`, not `\sum_k \lambda_k \cdot \inf_\gamma J^{(k)}(\gamma)`. So:

$$C_V(F_i, F_j) \;\geq\; \inf_\gamma \sum_k \lambda_k J^{(k)}(\gamma) \;\geq\; \max_k \lambda_k \cdot \inf_\gamma J^{(k)}(\gamma),$$

but **not** the sum of per-term infima in general.

**Corollary V-AFD-T15-cor (tighter individual bounds).**

(a) `C_V(F_i, F_j) \geq \lambda_E \cdot 0.0221β` (energy barrier dominant).
(b) `C_V(F_i, F_j) \geq \lambda_K \cdot 1` (K-jump dominant).
(c) `C_V(F_i, F_j) \geq \lambda_\tau \cdot d_B(\tau_{F_i}, \tau_{F_j})` (topology dominant).
(d) `C_V(F_i, F_j) \geq \lambda_D \cdot \|D_{F_i} - D_{F_j}\|_2` (diagnostic dominant).

The **strongest** bound is the *maximum* over (a)–(d), which is sharper than the sum when only one term dominates.

### D.5 Application: V-AFD-aware merge ordering

Under V-AFD-T15, the merge transition cost has multiple non-negative contributions. This gives a **multi-criteria preorder** on merge transitions:

> F_i → F_j is "cheaper" than F_i → F_k (both merges) if AND only if it is cheaper on each dominant criterion.

For example, two merges with the same energy barrier but different topology change (different `d_B(τ_{F_i}, τ_{F_j})`) are *distinguishable* via the τ-component of C_V.

This is **new content** beyond AFD-T7: AFD-T7 gives one number (the barrier lower bound). V-AFD-T15 gives a *vector of bounds* across the energy / K / τ / D / Len components, each individually meaningful.

## Part E — Cross-implications

### E.1 V-AFD-T13 + V-AFD-T14 combined

V-AFD-T13(a) says deterministic finite-time Z_+ is not Markov; V-AFD-T14(a) says Aut(G)-symmetric formations are Layer-2 invisible. Combined:

**Corollary E-1.** For Aut(G)-symmetric formation states, the *non-Markov-ness* of finite-time deterministic dynamics at the F_i / F_j level is also Aut(G)-invisible: the trajectories from B_{F_i} and from g · B_{F_i} = B_{F_j} are Aut(G)-equivalent under the natural action, so any non-Markov phenomenon is symmetric.

**Consequence.** The non-Markov-ness of T13(a) is "real" only for **topologically coincident** F_i / F_j (V-AFD-T9(b)(iii)), which is conjecturally absent in canonical SCC (V-AFD-T14(c)-conj). If the conjecture holds, deterministic V-AFD-T13(a) non-Markov-ness *collapses to zero* under the Aut(G)-quotient.

This is a striking result: **on V_form / Aut(G), deterministic vector dynamics may be approximately Markov in a non-trivial way**. Subject to V-AFD-T14(c)-conj. Open as OP-VAFD-003a (Markov on V_form / Aut(G)).

### E.2 V-AFD-T3-R + V-AFD-T15 combined

V-AFD-T3-R(c) gives local Lyapunov w(F) near each formation; V-AFD-T15 gives global merge cost lower bounds. Combined:

**Corollary E-2.** Near a formation F_i with local weights w(F_i), the loss `L_{w(F_i)}` decreases monotonically along gradient flow. Therefore: a merge transition F_i → F_j requires *climbing* L_{w(F_i)} (since gradient flow inside B_{F_i} cannot reach F_j). The climb is bounded below by the *L_w-version* of the barrier:

$$\sup_{\gamma \in \mathrm{Adm}(F_i, F_j)} \min_s L_{w(F_i)}(D(\gamma(s))) \;\geq\; L_{w(F_i)}(D(\gamma(s_*))) \;>\; L_{w(F_i)}(D(u_{F_i}^*)),$$

with strict inequality wherever γ enters B_{F_j}.

This is the V-AFD analog of "barrier ≥ depth of well", lifted to the loss-based Lyapunov.

**Status.** Corollary of T3-R(c) + T15. Cat B inheriting from both.

### E.3 V-AFD-T13(b) + V-AFD-T14 combined for small-T_*

V-AFD-T13(b) says small-T_* basin-label process is Markov; V-AFD-T14 says Aut(G)-symmetric formations are equivalent. Combined:

**Corollary E-3.** The small-T_* basin-label Markov chain is naturally defined on `V_form / Aut(G)` rather than V_form. Transition rates between Aut(G)-orbits are *symmetric* under the residual group action; the chain is Aut(G)-equivariant.

This is consistent with classical metastability theory in symmetric systems (Bovier-Den Hollander style).
